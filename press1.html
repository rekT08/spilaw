SPI-LAW Challenges Unconstitutional Provisions in Edo State Customary Court of Appeal (Re-establishment) Law, 2025 and Invalid Appointment of Acting President

September 28, 2026

The Incorporated Trustees of the Society of Public Interest Lawyers in Nigeria (SPI-LAW) has instituted Suit No. FHC/B/09/2026 at the Federal High Court, Benin Judicial Division, against the National Judicial Council, the Governor of Edo State, the Edo State House of Assembly, the Edo State Judicial Service Commission, the Customary Court of Appeal, Edo State, and Hon. Justice Mary Nekpen Asemota.

The action seeks the determination of fundamental constitutional questions arising from the Customary Court of Appeal (Re-establishment) Law of Edo State, 2025 and the purported appointment of an Acting President of the re-established Court.

Major Issues Raised

SPI-LAW contends that:

1. 1. Section 6(2) of the 2025 Law, which purports to confer *original jurisdiction* on the Customary Court of Appeal, Edo State over chieftaincy and traditional stool matters, is inconsistent with Section 282(1) of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 (as amended). The Constitution limits the Court to appellate and supervisory jurisdiction in civil proceedings involving questions of customary law. By virtue of Section 1(3) of the Constitution, the offending provision is null and void to the extent of its inconsistency.

2. The purported appointment of the 6th Defendant as Acting President of the Customary Court of Appeal, Edo State, is unconstitutional. Following the abolition of the Court in 2015, its former Judges (including the 6th Defendant) were formally re-appointed and sworn in as Judges of the High Court of Edo State. No person has since been recommended by the National Judicial Council and appointed as a Judge of the re-established Customary Court of Appeal in accordance with Section 281 of the Constitution. There is presently no duly constituted pool of serving Judges of the Customary Court of Appeal from which an Acting President can be validly selected under Section 281(4).

3. Section 55(1) of the 2025 Law, to the extent that it authorises the appointment of an Acting President from among former Judges of the defunct Court who are not presently serving Judges of the re-established Court, is unconstitutional, null and void.

Reliefs Sought

1. Declarations that the impugned provisions of the 2025 Law are unconstitutional and void;

2. An order striking down the offending portions of Sections 6(2) and 55(1);

3. An order setting aside the purported appointment of the 6th Defendant as Acting President and any other appointments made into the Bench of the Court in violation of Section 281;

4. Orders of restraint against further unconstitutional appointments or recognition of such appointees; and

5. An order directing the relevant Defendants to comply strictly with the constitutional procedure for the appointment of the President and Judges of the Customary Court of Appeal, Edo State.

“The Constitution is supreme. No State law or executive action can validly override the clear provisions of Sections 281 and 282. The Customary Court of Appeal is a superior court of record whose composition and jurisdiction are constitutionally regulated. Appointments cannot be made by administrative fiat or through transitional provisions that bypass the National Judicial Council. Public interest demands strict adherence to constitutional due process in the appointment of judicial officers and the delineation of judicial jurisdiction. This suit is instituted solely in furtherance of the rule of law, constitutionalism, and good governance.”

SPI-LAW remains committed to strategic public-interest litigation that promotes accountability, transparency, and fidelity to the Constitution.

Signed:

Sadiya B. Saleh

Director of Media & Publicity

SOCIETY OF PUBLIC INTEREST LAWYERS IN NIGERIA (SPI-LAW) FILES SUIT TO ENFORCE JUDICIAL FINANCIAL AUTONOMY IN EDO STATE: Suit No. FHC/B/CS/97/2026.

September 28, 2026

The Incorporated Trustees of Society of Public Interest Lawyers in Nigeria (SPI-LAW) instituted a constitutional action at the Federal High Court, Benin Judicial Division, on 4th September 2026.

Suit No. FHC/B/CS/97/2026, challenges the validity of the Edo State Judiciary Financial Autonomy Amendment Law 2023 which was signed into law on 24th August 2026.

An Ex Parte Motion seeking Interim Injunction was also filed to restrain the Defendants from taking steps under the Amendment Law pending the hearing of the Motion on Notice.

The Defendants

1. Governor of Edo State

2. Accountant-General of the Federation

3. National Judicial Council

4. Edo State House of Assembly

5. Accountant General of Edo State

SPI-LAW contends that the Amendment Law is inconsistent with Sections 121(1), (2) & (3) of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 (as amended). Specifically, the law subjects capital funds of the Edo State Judiciary to Executive proposals and approvals, thereby creating a dichotomy between “recurrent” and “capital” expenditure.

Questions for Determination

1. Whether the Accountant-General of the Federation can lawfully disburse Federal allocation to Edo State while the Amendment Law remains operative;

2. Whether the National Judicial Council can constitutionally consider and recommend appointments of judicial officers for Edo State while the Judiciary’s financial independence is undermined.

Section 121(3) of the Constitution mandates in clear terms that: "Any amount standing to the credit of the Judiciary of a State in the Consolidated Revenue Fund of the State shall be paid directly to the heads of the courts concerned."

SPI-LAW argues that the word “any” admits of no exception, and the phrase “shall be paid directly” leaves no room for executive control or legislative appropriation of judiciary funds.

Reliefs Sought

1. A Declaration that the Amendment Law is void for being inconsistent with Section 121(3) of the Constitution;

2. An Order setting aside and nullifying the Edo State Judiciary Financial Autonomy Amendment Law 2023;

3. An Order of Injunction restraining the Accountant-General of the Federation from disbursing any statutory allocation from the Federation Account to Edo State pending the determination of the suit;

4. An Order of Injunction restraining the National Judicial Council from considering or recommending appointments of judicial officers for Edo State pending the determination of the suit;

5. Perpetual Injunctions restraining the Governor, House of Assembly, AGF and others from implementing or enforcing the law, and from interfering with direct payment of judiciary funds;

6. ₦100,000,000.00 as costs of the action.

SPI-LAW has also filed a Motion on Notice for interlocutory injunction to preserve the status quo and prevent irreparable damage to judicial independence.

According to SPI-LAW, “The purpose of Section 121(3) is to secure financial independence of the Judiciary. To allow Executive proposals and approvals on judiciary capital funds is to subject the third arm of government to the purse-strings of the Executive, in direct breach of the doctrine of separation of powers.”

The Society relies on settled authorities including Olisa Agbakoba v. AGF and John Aikpokpo-Martins & Olukunle Edun v. Governor of Delta State & Ors (Suit No. A/164/2022, judgment of 7 March 2023) which held that Governors lack vires to impose conditions on judiciary disbursements.

SPI-LAW reaffirms its commitment to the promotion of good governance, the rule of law, and the protection of public interest in Nigeria. We urge all stakeholders to respect the Constitution and allow the Court to determine this matter of urgent public importance.

Signed:

Sadiya B. Saleh

Director of Media & Publicity


RE: THE PURPORTED CONFERMENT OF A CHIEFTAINCY TITLE ON THE HONOURABLE CHIEF JUDGE OF LAGOS STATE, HON. JUSTICE KAZEEM OLANREWAJU ALOGBA BY THE AYANGBURE OF IKORODU KINGDOM: PUTTING THE RECORD STRAIGHT.

September 28, 2026

The Society of Public Interest Lawyers in Nigeria (SPI-LAW) considers it necessary to put the records straight regarding recent reports concerning the purported conferment and acceptance of the chieftaincy title of Bamofin of Ikorodu Kingdom on the Honourable Chief Judge of Lagos State, Hon. Justice Kazeem Olanrewaju Alogba.

The matter arose following a publication by Impact Newspaper dated 8th August, 2026, reporting that His Royal Majesty, Oba [Dr.] Kabir Adewale Shotobi, the Ayangbure of Ikorodu, had announced the conferment of the title of Bamofin of Ikorodu Kingdom on the Honourable Chief Judge during a royal visit to senior citizens in Ikorodu.

Of particular concern to SPI-LAW was the report that the Honourable Chief Judge had accepted the purported conferment by receiving royal beads, especially in view of Rule 13.2 of the Code of Conduct for Judicial Officers of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, which provides that: “A Judicial Officer shall not take or accept any Chieftaincy title while in office.”

In furtherance of its mandate to promote the rule of law, good governance, transparency, accountability and the ethical conduct of public officers, SPI-LAW took the following steps:

1. SPI-LAW sought official clarification

Rather than rely solely on the newspaper publication or draw conclusions from the report and accompanying photograph, SPI-LAW formally wrote to the Honourable Chief Judge on 1st September, 2026, through the Chief Registrar of the High Court of Lagos State.

In that letter, SPI-LAW respectfully requested clarification as to whether the publication accurately represented what transpired, particularly the allegation that the Honourable Chief Judge had accepted the chieftaincy title by receiving royal beads.

SPI-LAW made clear that the request was made in good faith and in the interest of preserving public confidence in the Judiciary and ensuring that the records were put straight.

The Honourable Chief Judge subsequently responded by a letter dated 9th September, 2026, which was received by SPI-LAW on 15th September, 2026.

In his response, the Honourable Chief Judge provided a clear and unequivocal account of what transpired. His Lordship stated that when His Royal Majesty announced the title of Bamofin of Ikorodu, he immediately informed the Royal Father that, as a serving Judicial Officer, he could not assume or take any chieftaincy title until after his retirement from service.

His Lordship further expressly stated that the report that he accepted the conferment by receiving royal beads was completely untrue.

According to the clarification, His Lordship subsequently visited the Palace as a matter of courtesy and again reiterated his position that, as a serving Judicial Officer, he could not accept or assume the chieftaincy title.

His Royal Majesty, according to the response, graciously accepted the position and rested the matter.

The Honourable Chief Judge therefore made his position unequivocal: he did not accept or assume the chieftaincy title of Bamofin of Ikorodu Kingdom.

Upon receipt of the Honourable Chief Judge’s response, SPI-LAW wrote to His Lordship on 16th September, 2026, formally acknowledging the clarification.

SPI-LAW expressed its appreciation for the prompt response and, particularly, for the fact that the clarification was personally signed by the Honourable Chief Judge.

Having received the clarification, SPI-LAW informed His Lordship that the concerns raised in its earlier letter had been adequately addressed.

SPI-LAW considers it important that the public record accurately reflects the sequence of events. The initial newspaper report gave the impression that the Honourable Chief Judge had accepted the chieftaincy title and received the royal beads as evidence of such acceptance. That report raised a legitimate issue requiring clarification because of the applicable ethical rule governing Judicial Officers.

SPI-LAW therefore sought clarification directly from the Honourable Chief Judge rather than speculate or make an allegation without first giving His Lordship an opportunity to respond.

The Honourable Chief Judge has now expressly clarified that he did not accept the chieftaincy title, did not assume the title, and did not accept the royal beads as an acceptance of the purported conferment.

His Lordship also explained that he had expressly communicated his inability, as a serving Judicial Officer, to accept or assume such a title.

Accordingly, SPI-LAW considers the matter addressed.

SPI-LAW wishes to emphasise that its intervention was not intended to impugn the integrity of the Honourable Chief Judge or to cast aspersions on the Judiciary.

Our intervention was motivated by the need to ensure that matters touching on the ethical conduct of Judicial Officers are properly clarified where circumstances arise that could reasonably create public concern or misunderstanding.

The Judiciary is a vital institution in a constitutional democracy, and public confidence in its independence, integrity and adherence to ethical standards is of fundamental importance.

It is in that spirit that SPI-LAW will continue to engage constructively, responsibly and respectfully with public institutions whenever issues within its mandate arise.

We commend the Honourable Chief Judge for providing the requested clarification and for affirming, by his stated conduct, his understanding of the ethical restriction applicable to serving Judicial Officers.

For the avoidance of doubt, and based on the official clarification furnished to SPI-LAW by the Honourable Chief Judge of Lagos State: Hon. Justice Kazeem Olanrewaju Alogba did not accept or assume the chieftaincy title of Bamofin of Ikorodu Kingdom.

SPI-LAW has received and acknowledged that clarification and considers it necessary to make the position known publicly so that the earlier report does not continue to create a contrary impression.

SPI-LAW remains committed to its mandate of promoting the rule of law, good governance, transparency, accountability, institutional integrity and the ethical conduct of public officers, while ensuring that its interventions are undertaken fairly, responsibly and in accordance with the principles of natural justice.

Signed:

Sadiya B. Saleh

Director of Media & Publicity


SPI-LAW SUES THE FEDERAL GOVERNMENT AND THE CONCESSIONAIRE OVER BENIN–ASABA EXPRESSWAY; SEEKS CANCELLATION OF CONCESSION AND URGENT REMEDIAL WORKS

September 28, 2026

The Society of Public Interest Lawyers in Nigeria (SPI-LAW) has instituted a public interest action at the Federal High Court, Abuja Judicial Division, over the deteriorating condition of the Benin–Asaba Expressway and the alleged failure to ensure the safety and proper management of the strategic highway.

The action, Suit No. FHC/ABJ/CS/2171/2026, was filed on 11 September 2026 against the following:

The Defendants

1. Benin–Asaba Expressway Concession Company Limited;

2. The Attorney-General of the Federation;

3. The Honourable Minister of Works, Federal Republic of Nigeria.

Reliefs Sought

1. An order cancelling/terminating the concession agreement relating to the Benin–Asaba Expressway on the grounds stated in the substantive suit;

2. An order directing the Federal Government to assume appropriate control and take remedial measures necessary to restore the highway to a safe and serviceable condition;

3. An order compelling urgent rehabilitation, reconstruction, and remedial works on the affected portions of the Benin–Asaba Expressway;

4. Orders for appropriate traffic management and public safety measures pending comprehensive rehabilitation of the highway.

SPI-LAW has also filed applications seeking urgent interim and interlocutory mandatory orders for immediate measures to protect motorists and other road users from the continuing dangers posed by the severely damaged, excavated, and deteriorated portions of the highway.

SPI-LAW maintains that the condition of the highway is not merely a private contractual disagreement between a concessionaire and the Federal Government. It raises fundamental questions concerning the Government’s constitutional responsibility to protect the lives, safety, and welfare of Nigerians.

Section 14(2)(b) of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 (as amended), expressly provides: “the security and welfare of the people shall be the primary purpose of government.”

SPI-LAW considers this constitutional provision particularly relevant. Where a major federal highway is allegedly left in a condition that exposes road users to avoidable risks of accidents, injury, loss of life, economic hardship, and security threats, the Federal Government cannot stand aside simply because there is a dispute concerning the performance of a concession agreement.

The Constitution further provides in Section 13 that it is the duty and responsibility of all organs of government and authorities exercising legislative, executive, or judicial powers to conform to, observe, and apply the provisions of Chapter II of the Constitution.

SPI-LAW therefore contends that the Federal Government has a continuing constitutional responsibility to act in the public interest and to take reasonable steps to protect Nigerians using this critical transportation corridor.

The Society emphasises that the public should not become casualties of a contractual dispute between the Government and a concessionaire. The Benin–Asaba Expressway is a critical economic and transportation link. Its continued deterioration affects motorists, businesses, traders, farmers, transport operators, emergency services, and the wider Nigerian economy.

SPI-LAW has accordingly approached the Federal High Court for judicial intervention, including the cancellation of the concession and urgent remedial measures to restore safe passage along the highway.

The Society remains committed to accountability in the management of public infrastructure and will pursue this action in the public interest and for the protection of millions of Nigerians who depend on the Benin–Asaba Expressway.

Signed:

Sadiya B. Saleh

Director of Media & Publicity


STATEMENT BY THE SOCIETY OF PUBLIC INTEREST LAWYERS IN NIGERIA (SPI-LAW) RE: ALLEGATION OF DEFILEMENT AGAINST GODSTIME ONYEMECHARA UFUOMA

September 20, 2026

The Society of Public Interest Lawyers in Nigeria (SPI-LAW) has become aware of the allegation and criminal charge of defilement made against Mr. Godstime Onyemechara Ufuoma, arising from an allegation concerning a six-year-old child in Warri, Delta State.

Following a review of the circumstances surrounding the allegation and the information presently available to us, SPI-LAW has serious concerns about the case and has formed the view that there are serious doubts about the guilt of Mr. Ufuoma. Consequently, SPI-LAW has resolved to undertake his defence and provide him with legal representation throughout the proceedings.

Mr. Ufuoma was a National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) member serving as a teacher at a school in Warri. He was provided accommodation within the school premises and, according to the information available to us, his commitment and conduct were such that the school retained him as a teacher after the completion of his service.

There was an arrangement at the school whereby, after the teachers had closed for the day, Mr. Ufuoma, being resident on the premises, would remain available to open the school gate for parents who arrived late to collect their children, and thereafter secure the premises. It was in this context, according to the account available to SPI-LAW, that a parent who arrived late one day in May questioned the presence of a male teacher living within the school premises and why a male teacher would be left around children after the other teachers had departed.

On 3 September 2026, Mr. Ufuoma was arrested by the police and informed that a complaint had been made against him alleging that, sometime in May, he had defiled the complainant's six-year-old daughter. Mr. Ufuoma has consistently denied the allegation. From the time of his arrest, he has maintained that he did not commit the offence and that he has no knowledge of the alleged incident.

SPI-LAW is particularly concerned by the account that, following his arrest, Mr. Ufuoma was allegedly subjected to repeated pressure to admit to an offence he consistently denied committing. He has stated that he was encouraged to accept responsibility on the assurance that the matter would be settled if he did so. He refused, maintaining that he could not confess to an offence he did not commit.

We are also concerned by the circumstances surrounding the taking and subsequent publication of a photograph of Mr. Ufuoma while he was handcuffed. According to his account, an individual identified as Kelvin Ejumudo, who described himself as a human right activist, allegedly took the photograph while Mr. Ufuoma was in police custody.

The photograph subsequently appeared on social media and was circulated to bloggers. A statement attributed to Mr. Ejumudo also publicly described Mr. Ufuoma as a “pedophile”, notwithstanding that the allegation against him had not been determined by any court.

SPI-LAW considers it fundamentally important to emphasise that an allegation is not a conviction. Every person accused of a criminal offence is entitled to the presumption of innocence and to a fair hearing in accordance with the constitution and applicable laws. The determination of criminal guilt belongs to a competent court, after due process and upon the evidence properly presented before it.

We are further concerned by the circumstances of Mr. Ufuoma's detention. He was reportedly held at Ekpan Police Station for six days before being remanded to Okere Correctional Centre, Warri, on 8 September 2026. He has stated that during his period at the police station he slept on the bare floor.

On 18 September 2026, the matter came before the Court for review of the remand order and our lawyer was in court to represent Mr. Ufuoma. The Court subsequently adjourned the matter to 6 October 2026 for review of the remand order. Mr. Ufuoma remains in custody.

SPI-LAW has deliberately refrained from identifying the child involved in the allegation, in keeping with the need to protect the identity and privacy of a minor.

We recognise the seriousness of every allegation involving a child and the importance of appropriate investigation and protection of children. At the same time, the seriousness of an allegation cannot justify the abandonment of due process, the presumption of innocence, or the right of an accused person to a fair trial.

SPI-LAW will therefore undertake the defence of Mr. Godstime Onyemechara Ufuoma and ensure that his constitutional and legal rights are protected throughout the proceedings. We call on all persons and organisations commenting on this matter to exercise restraint, avoid prejudging the case, and allow the judicial process to take its course. In particular, persons should refrain from publicly branding an accused person with criminal labels before a court has determined the allegation.

SPI-LAW will continue to monitor the proceedings and will take all appropriate legal steps necessary to protect the rights of Mr. Ufuoma and ensure that the matter is determined fairly and in accordance with the law.

Signed:

Sadiya B. Saleh

Director of Media & Publicity

Justice for Mr. Adeda must not be negotiated, diluted or buried under bureaucratic procedures.

SPI-LAW FILES DISCIPLINARY PETITION AGAINST ABUBAKAR MALAMI, SAN, FORMER ATTORNEY-GENERAL OF THE FEDERATION, AT THE LEGAL PRACTITIONERS DISCIPLINARY COMMITTEE - BBLPDC/2196/2026

September 29, 2026

The Society of Public Interest Lawyers in Nigeria [SPI-LAW] on 28th September, 2026, filed a formal Petition for professional misconduct and infamous conduct in a professional respect against Abubakar Malami, SAN, former Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, at the Legal Practitioners Disciplinary Committee [LPDC], Body of Benchers, Abuja.

The Petition is registered as Case No: BBLPDC/2196/2026 – Incorporated Trustees of the Society of Public Interest Lawyers in Nigeria v. Abubakar Malami, SAN.

The Petition is founded on the damning, detailed and reasoned findings made against Mr. Malami, SAN, by an International Chamber of Commerce [ICC] Arbitral Tribunal in its Final Award dated 16th September, 2026 in ICC Case No. 26260/SPN/AB/CPB: Sunrise Power and Transmission Company Limited & Anor v. Federal Government of Nigeria

The Tribunal, composed of Ms. Melanie van Leeuwen, Mr. Simon Nesbitt KC, and Prof. Stavros Brekoulakis, with seat in Paris, France, heard evidence from high-ranking Nigerian officials including former President Muhammadu Buhari, GCFR.[President]

In that Award, after a full adversarial hearing, the Tribunal made the following findings concerning the Respondent:

1. Lack of Authority: That Mr. Malami, SAN, while serving as Attorney-General, together with the then Minister of Power, lacked both actual and apparent/ostensible authority to execute the Settlement Agreement of 21st January, 2020 and Addendum of 25th March, 2020 on behalf of Nigeria [Award Paras 785-786].

2. Product of Corruption: That the Settlement Instruments “are a product of corruption” and are unenforceable for violating Nigerian public policy [Award Paras 1405, 1408]. The Tribunal accepted testimony that Mr. Malami directly solicited a bribe and claimed a portion of the settlement sum payable to Sunrise [Paras 1352, 1354].

3. Corrupt Deal: That an adverse inference was drawn that “a corrupt deal was in fact reached between Mr. Adesanya, on the one hand, and Attorney-General Malami on the other” involving a promise to pay the Respondent a share of the settlement sum in exchange for committing Nigeria to the instruments [Paras 1403-1404, 1407].

4. Acting Against Nigeria's Interest: That as Chief Law Officer, Mr. Malami “deliberately acted against the best interests of Nigeria” and “has not been negotiating on behalf of Nigeria... but for Sunrise,” motivated by “other incentive(s)” [Para 1374].

5. Inappropriate and Corrupt Relationship: That there existed “an inappropriate relation” between Mr. Adesanya and Mr. Malami characterized by “a high degree of trust and... a lack of moral compass” and that he was actively applying his efforts to ensure “that we get paid” [Para 1394], with red flags “indicative of a corrupt relation” [Para 1402].

6. Disregard for Administration of Justice: That despite being present in Paris during the hearing and meeting with his father-in-law, former President Buhari, Mr. Malami refused to appear for cross-examination as a key witness called by Nigeria. The Tribunal recorded its “serious dissatisfaction with the disregard that both Mr. Malami and Mr. Aondoakaa have displayed for the administration of justice” [Paras 1000-1001]

The Allegations

In view of these findings, SPI-LAW has asked the LPDC to determine whether the Respondent is guilty of infamous conduct in a professional respect contrary to Rule 1 of the Rules of Professional Conduct for Legal Practitioners, 2023 - a Rule incidentally signed into law by the Respondent himself as Attorney-General - which requires every lawyer to uphold the rule of law, maintain the highest standard of conduct, and not engage in conduct unbecoming of a legal practitioner; and Section 11 of the Legal Practitioners Act.

The particulars include solicitation of bribe/corrupt deal while serving as AGF, deliberately acting against the interest of his client, the Federal Republic of Nigeria, maintaining an inappropriate relationship with a party adverse to Nigeria, executing instruments without authority, and showing contempt for the administration of justice.

The Originating Application is supported by Affidavits deposed to by John Aikpokpo-Martins, Esq., President of SPI-LAW, and Olukunle O. Edun, SAN, Vice President, with a flash drive containing the Final Award [Exhibit SPI-LAW 1] and certified extracts of the material findings [Exhibit SPI-LAW 2].

Why SPI-LAW is Acting

The office of Attorney-General of the Federation is the highest legal office in the land. Its occupant must embody absolute integrity, loyalty to the Federation, and fidelity to the rule of law. When an international tribunal makes such grave findings against a former occupant of that office, public confidence in the legal profession in Nigeria, both locally and internationally, is severely threatened.

SPI-LAW brings this Petition in the public interest pursuant to Rule 4 of the LPDC Rules, 2020. We believe that no one, no matter how highly placed, is above professional accountability. Failure to call the Respondent to answer would send a catastrophic message that high office confers immunity from the ethics of the Bar.

We urge the LPDC, as the statutory guardian of the honour and integrity of the profession, to certify that there is a case to answer and to invite Mr. Malami, SAN, to defend the allegations. We emphasize that these are allegations for disciplinary inquiry based on the Tribunal's findings; the LPDC will afford the Respondent full fair hearing as required by law.

The law must be no respecter of persons.
